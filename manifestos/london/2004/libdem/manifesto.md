# A SAFER GREATER LONDON

London has the highest rate of recorded crime in England. Not surprisingly Londoners are more concerned about crime than anything else. Although stable, overall levels of crime remain high, and violent crime has risen over the last four years. Crucially, Londoners' fear of crime is growing, which is an alarming trend.

Liberal Democrats believe in the need to reform the Metropolitan Police Service, and placing local policing and local priorities at the top of their agenda. Through guaranteeing a minimum number of officers for each neighbourhood, we can create a safer London where people feel secure in their own communities. It is crucial that we continue to devote sufficient resources to tackling serious crime and the threat of terrorism.

## SAFETY

### A Permanent Community Police Service
Liberal Democrats will insist that every neighbourhood (com-munities of 5–10,000) in London is provided with its own designated ‘community police force’ by 2006.

This will grow over the four-year term to comprise four police of-ficers and four community support officers, who will not be removed from their local duties except in extraordinary circumstances.

A sergeant on a 4-year contract will head each ‘community police force’ so they can get to know and establish links with the local community. It is a priority for Liberal Democrats that London-ers feel safe and confident in and around their own homes. These locally ‘owned’ officers will become familiar faces within their work neighbourhoods. They would agree their work targets and shift patterns with ward council-lors to ensure that their efforts are focused around community priorities. Each unit will be contactable by their local commu-nity 7 days a week.

The new arrangements will play a key role in establishing trust and confidence in street level policing, and help to reduce public disorder and the fear of crime.

Initially all police officers will come from redeployment; Police Community Support Officers (PCSOs) will come from redeploy-ment with additional PCSOs fund-ed through efficiency savings.

### Police Recruitment
Liberal Democrats continue to support the increase in the Metropolitan Police (Met) num-bers. Our overall target is 35,000 uniformed officers. It is important that the police are representative of London’s diverse community; we will work closely with the Met to promote the recruitment of under-represented groups.

In particular we will work with the Met to increase the numbers of special constables in London. Special constables play an important part in policing, bringing with them important community links and specialist knowledge.

### Safer Trains, Safer Stations
We will enter into immediate negotiations with the Rail Authori-ties, train operating companies and local boroughs to highlight Londoners’ concerns over safety, and campaign for:
1. CCTV to be installed in and around all London stations.
2. All stations to have clearly visible and sensibly sited emer-gency phone points so passengers can summon help and report vandalism.
3. All stations to be kept in a good state of repair, with graffiti and litter removed regularly.

We will immediately enter into discussions with the Met and BTP to identify ways in which to increase a visible police presence on trains including best use of PCSOs and special constables, especially after dark.

We aim to provide a police presence at all major Tube, train and bus stations during rush hours and other appropriate times.

### Better Customer Service
We will examine – on a case-by-case basis – the arguments for re-opening police reception at stations that are currently closed to the public, as well as re-locating inappropriate and inaccessible sta-tions to more publicly convenient positions, such as close to train stations or shopping centres.

Our revolutionary One Number for London would provide a non-emergency information and advice service for Londoners, with the ability to connect Londoners di-rectly to their local police station if necessary.

### Local Accountability
We aim to publish borough-by-borough crime statistics every 3 months.

We plan to organise the pro-duction of annual ‘community safety reports’, for all areas of London, to include local crime pre-vention information, reporting and conviction figures; crime rates in each ward (broken down into each major class of crime), the work of the probation service (including the frequency, duration and nature of community service in each area) and local courts; and an overview of significant crime prevention work that is taking place.

We plan to complement each community safety report with an annual public meeting, at which those responsible for crime prevention and reduction would be held to account by the local community. The local Borough Police Commander, the Leader of the local Borough Council, the local councillor responsi-ble for community safety, senior Probation and Courts officers, the relevant London Assembly Member and the local constitu-ency MP, will all be expected to attend and held accountable at these borough meetings.

Police and the voluntary sector to look at new ways of tackling youth crime, such as additional construc-tive leisure opportunities and improved recreational facilities for young people in each borough of London.

### Making Offenders Serve the Community
If community service is to be of true and worthwhile benefit, con-victed offenders must undertake tasks that address the needs and priorities of the community that has been affected by their crimes.

Those responsible for damaging our local communities should pay them back. We will work with the London boroughs, the Courts, the Youth Service and the Probation Service to develop local input in the setting of community service work, so that victims and commu-nities can influence the way that offenders pay back their debt to society.

### Discrimination and Hate Crimes
All hate crimes whether based on race, religion or sexual ori-entation are unacceptable. The Liberal Democrats will continue to promote greater tolerance and un-derstanding, and will work closely with the police to ensure that tack-ling hate crimes remains a priority and that those responsible for such crimes are brought to justice.

### London’s Fire and Rescue Service
We will work with fire fighters to provide a twenty-first century ser-vice for Londoners, and to ensure the service is adequately prepared for a major terrorist incident.

## TACKLING TERRORISM
*   Start immediate talks with the Government for an increase in the civil defence budget for London.
*   Work with central Government to provide better information to the public on what to do in the event of a major terrorist attack, with the view to including this information with Londoners' annual Council Tax bills.
*   Facilitate closer working of our police and security services at an EU level and with other capital cities, sharing security information where appropriate, and disseminating best practice in combating terrorism.
*   Suspend talks on installing mobile phone access on the deep line Tube network, due to the recent history of using mobile phones to detonate terrorist bombs.
*   Consult on the establishment of a dedicated London Emergency Volunteer Reserve Force to assist the emergency services in case of an attack.
*   Enter into immediate discussions to arrange the merger of the British Transport Police (BTP) in London with the Metropolitan Police Service. In order to provide more effective co-ordination of security on London's transport infrastructure.

# TRANSPORT FOR A GREATER LONDON

Any recent transport gains have come despite the Mayor's incompetence and the Labour government's obstruction. Transport for London's financial planning is chaotic; consistently failing to spend their budget yet wanting more for the following year. At the point where this year's budget was being planned, Transport for London had failed to spend a staggering £345 million. The Mayor said congestion charging would raise £120 million a year – the reality has been £68 million. Customer service was so bad the Mayor had to hand another £31 million to Capita. Bus subsidies are rocketing.

Labour's insistence on their ruinous plan to hand Tube main-tenance to private companies cost £500 million in legal costs alone. Labour Ministers dither on vital projects like Crossrail, the East London line extensions and Thameslink 2000.

Liberal Democrats aim to lift transport standards right across Greater London, not just in the centre, so our city can function as efficiently as many of our European neighbours’. Life needs to be better for cyclists and pe-destrians. Those without a Tube network need better rail services, integrated with bus and tram. We need to add value to Labour's foolish PPP for the Tube so we get earlier upgrades of track and signalling. A Liberal Democrat Mayor will insist on competent financial management so that all the policies set out below become a reality over the next four years.

## TUBES

### Late Night Tube
We will extend the running times of the Tube network until 2 am on Thursday, Friday and Sat-urday nights, allowing Londoners to enjoy a longer night out without worrying how to get home safely. This will give a much-needed boost to London's entertainment and hospitality industry.

### Tube Delivery Pledge
We will monitor and publish 5 key performance indicators on the Tube – overcrowding, safety, trains per hour (peak and off-peak), wait-ing times and cleanliness – and make them available to Tube users in stations and online.

### Travel Information
We plan to provide a clear and consistent display of travel infor-mation across the Tube network. Tube drivers and other London Underground staff should inform passengers about problems on all ongoing public transport connec-tions.

## Reduce Overcrowding
We will look at proposals to help overcrowding, through changes to the fare structure to encourage travel outside peak times.

## Increased Reliability
We will immediately start discussions with all interested par-ties to work out the best ways of speeding up track and signalling renewal and repair by extended maintenance, at times which cause least disruption.

## TRAINS

### London Commuter Service
London's commuters need real political leadership from the new Mayor to integrate com-muter rail services with existing Tube, tram, DLR and bus services. Although the Tube, tram, DLR and bus networks are managed by Transport for London, Lon-don's railways are overseen by the Strategic Rail Authority, which too often gives preference to inter-city services. We will undertake a wide-ranging consultation on the creation of a London Commuter Service, to give Londoners an effective voice in railway planning throughout Greater London. So decisions that affect London are made by Londoners.

We will work with the Train Operating Companies and Network Rail to ensure that all stations have adequate facilities – all stations should be equipped with an inquiry point phone, along with debit- and credit-card ticket machines.

## WALKING

People frequently use the Tube for short journeys because they’re not sure how to get to their destination on foot, or how long the walk will take. For many of these journeys, it would be quicker, cheaper and healthier to travel on foot. In other cases people are put off by London's pedestrian-unfriendly roads.

We will ensure a pedestrian friendly Oxford Street by 2008, and consult on delivering im-proved transport hubs for buses, and an extra spur on the proposed new North-South tramline.

We will work with Transport for London towards ensuring better signage for pedestrians along key walking routes, showing typical walking times e.g. ‘Kew Gardens 10 minutes’, ‘Liverpool Street Station 8 minutes’. This will en-courage people – and especially tourists – to save time & money by travelling on foot, instead of adding to overcrowding on the Tube and buses.

We will ask Transport for London to investigate the feasi-bility of maps and signs at Tube stations to clearly indicate tube stations within a short walk of each other, and additional signs to help people work out the direction of their onward journey when they arrive at Tube stations.

## BUSES

### 21st Century Buses
We will examine the potential for cost savings and performance improvements that could result from using Global Positioning Satellite technology to track and manage bus movements. This has been already shown to work successfully in Brighton. We will work towards the replacement of the outdated and ineffective ‘Countdown’ technology to pro-vide accurate real time informa-tion to passengers at bus stops.

## Passenger Information
We will ask Transport for London to investigate announcement systems on buses, so each stop is clearly identified to passengers (as it is on the Tube) and onward connections to rail, Tube or tram are advertised.

### School Buses
We will consider pilot schemes in four London Boroughs (two in outer London, two in inner London) to get children to school on American-style Yellow School Buses. School buses are a safe and efficient means of transport, helping to cut road congestion and reduce air pollution.

## Roads
### Black Cabs
Black taxi cabs are an integral part of London Transport. Black cab drivers will be invited to elect a representative to be a full voting member on Transport for London.

### Parking Charter
We will develop a parking charter to promote a fairer and more consistent approach to parking enforcement across Greater London. We aim to include a common parking scheme for disabled people across Greater London.

## Congestion Charging
Liberal Democrats support the principle of Congestion Charging in central London, and are committed to making it work better for all Londoners. We will:
*   Allow an extra day to pay the congestion charge (extend the time limit from 10pm on the day to midnight on the following day).
*   Change the end of the charging period from 6.30pm to 5pm.
*   Allow advance block purchasing of tickets, without designating the dates in advance.
*   For all businesses, allow companies to register so that they can phone up to check if one of their vehicles has been in the congestion charging zone that day. For small businesses, we will reduce the minimum eligible size for 'fleet tickets' from 15 to 5 vehicles.
*   Not extend the Congestion Charging Zone westwards into Kensington & Chelsea.
*   Only consider expansion of the zone in boroughs already partly covered by the zone. Decision would ultimately be decided by a vote in a local ballot of registered electors in the possible extension areas.
*   Examine new categories for exemption.
*   Automatically give every vehicle 5 free entries into the congestion-charging zone each year.
*   The congestion charging zone will be suspended between Christmas and New Year.

### Instant Enforcement Pledge
Instant removal of all vehicles parked in bus lanes.

### Bus Lanes
We will allow motorbikes, scooters, cyclists and black cabs to use all bus lanes, at all times.

### Transport Links
We will look at the feasibility of establishing a system of taxi shares to take people – particularly the elderly – back from out of town shopping centres and supermarkets. We will also work with key stakeholders towards the establishment of secure late-night waiting areas at tube and train stations, and work with transport companies to promote co-ordinated late-night taxi-share schemes. This is aimed at making people, particularly women, less vulnerable.

## Cycling
### 'Take a Bike' Scheme
We will ask Transport for London to examine the introduction of a scheme to make thousands of bicycles available at a nominal hire charge at up to 50 locations across London. People will register their credit card details and pick up a bike in one place, and drop it off within a given time period at any 'Take A Bike' location.

### Protected Cycle Routes
We will put forward proposals for key cycle routes through central London – physically protected from other road users – marked with clear signposting and colour coding, and advertised by freely available Tube style maps.

### Security
We will work with key stakeholders to increase the number of secure, covered cycle racks in strategic central London locations – especially at rail termini and key transport interchanges. We will use consistent and proven designs that make parking and locking easier and more secure.

## The River
The Thames is a greatly underused and overlooked asset to London, and, with appropriate planning and guidance from the GLA, much better commercial use could be made of the river. In particular we will explore expanding the use of the river for carrying freight and waste/rubbish.

With the huge planned developments of the Thames Gateway region in the next few years, thousands of new buildings and many new roads will be constructed around the river.

The Liberal Democrats believe that every effort should be made to use the river as a means for servicing the construction of these new developments, taking the pressure off our overstretched roads, and minimising their environmental impact.

We will also work with the London Port Authority to find a

suitable location for a cruise ship terminal. We will encourage the development of commuter boat services, and we plan to integrate the ticketing of these with the rest of transport for London.

## CROSSRAIL

The Crossrail One project is vital to the development of London's economy. It will create £20 billion in wealth with a tax gain of £7.6 billion for the Treasury. Liberal Democrats are fully committed to the project. The City and Canary Wharf need a direct rail connection to Heathrow and to Eurostar at Stratford International. The new regeneration areas of East London in the Thames Gateway must be linked up from Shenfield and from Ebbsfleet through the Royal Docks. We do, however, have serious reservations about the proposed spur using the District Line through Richmond.

The Government must give the go-ahead to build the core Paddington-Liverpool Street-Canary Wharf section now.

Crossrail depends on new central London tunnels and work needs to start. We propose an enhanced specification for the twin tunnels so that trains from the West and East of England can use them – e.g. Bristol direct to the City, Ipswich direct to Paddington. This is vital too for our Heathrow-Stansted shuttle.

## AIRPORTS

Liberal Democrats remain totally opposed to any further development at Heathrow Airport and the proposed end to the runway alternation policy. The residents of West London who live under the flight paths have suffered enough. This is not a part of London that needs more runway capacity.

By 2007 Eurostar will reduce London-Paris journey times to 2 hours 15 minutes. We need a serious challenge to short haul air flights to the Continent, which crowd our skies. The next Mayor needs to campaign for sustainable aviation, promoting EU and international co-operation to ensure airlines meet their full external costs. Liberal Democrats want Britain to play a full part in the Trans-European transport network (TEN-T), working to develop high-speed rail links between the Channel Tunnel and the rest of the UK, and across all of Europe.

The Liberal Democrats believe that if any additional runway capacity is genuinely needed, it should be built conveniently close to the Thames Gateway area at Stansted. We propose a Heathrow-Stansted high-speed rail shuttle via the City and Stratford International, using the Crossrail infrastructure.

# HOUSING GREATER LONDON

The shortage of housing in London is a well-known crisis. Best estimates are that the capital requires 33,600 new homes every year to cope with the existing backlog of need and the capital's rapidly swelling population. Of this total, over 25,000 homes will need to be in the affordable bracket if individuals on lower incomes, many of whom work in key public services, are able to afford their own home.

The severe shortage of housing has caused London property prices to rocket – they have risen by 51% in the past three years alone. The average price of a house in London is now nearly £93,000 more than the UK average and over 9 times the aver-age salary of a nurse or firefighter in the capital. It is a simple fact that house prices are increasing faster than public sector pay and, consequently, public employ-ees are being priced out of the housing market.

Under the current Mayor’s term of office, homelessness in London has risen by 29% - this amounts to approximately 14,000 households. The capital now has nearly 63,000 families who are forced to live in temporary accommodation. London also has over 150,000 homes which are overcrowded – far higher than an-ywhere else in the country. Mean-while, the scandal is that there are an estimated 100,000 homes lying empty across London that could be brought back into use to help alleviate this huge problem. The current Mayor has done little to meet this challenge.

The short supply of housing, and affordable homes in particu-lar, has become a huge constraint on London’s economic growth. This is fundamentally responsible for exacerbating the recruitment and retention difficulties being faced by both the public and private sectors. Despite this wor-rying situation the current Mayor has not made housing provision a priority. He has done too little and left it too late. Liberal Demo-crats will not allow this crisis to continue. London’s economy and the prosperity and happiness of its residents are dependent on a steady supply of housing, which we will ensure becomes a reality.

The central issue behind Lon-don’s housing crisis is the severe shortage in supply of housing stock, coupled with high demand fuelling rising prices. This is largely due to long delays in plan-ning slowing down the supply of housing coming on stream. We will pursue a series of policies aimed at increasing the supply of housing in London.

### Protecting Private Tenants

We want to support and protect those renting properties in London and guard against the activities of unscrupulous landlords. We will conduct negotiations with Borough colleagues, encouraging them to establish a register of approved private sector landlords and their properties. These lists could also record complaints and issues, securing quality of accommoda-tion and treatment for tenants across London.

### More Investment for Housing

We will vigorously press the Government for more investment in London housing, making it

### Fast Track home building

Liberal Democrats will work with retailers and transport companies to identify unused urban space above and around supermarkets and train stations for affordable housing. We will set an overall long-term objective in the London Plan to generate 10,000 extra units through these Fast Track schemes. We will also conduct an annual review of affordable housing provision, with a view to setting accelerating annual delivery targets.

### More Responsibility for Housing

As part of our ‘Better Govern-ment for London’ agenda, we will lobby the Government to devolve strategic responsibility for housing to the Greater London Authority. This will allow for London-wide co-ordination of policy and will give the GLA a remit which is at least equal to that proposed for the other English regional assemblies. With our proposed abolition of the Government Office for London we will also lobby for its planning responsibili-ties to be devolved to the GLA.

### 50% Affordable Housing

We will ensure that, London-wide, 50% of all additional housing provision is affordable. However, we will not pursue the ‘one-size fits all’ approach of the current Mayor. We prefer individual site flexibility to ensure that housing schemes are relevant and achievable in their different localities.

### Homelessness Information Service

We intend to set up, in con-junction with Borough Councils, the establishment of a London-wide Homelessness Information Service. This will provide relevant information to those currently or soon-to-be affected by homeless-ness. We want this to be linked with empty homes strategies to ensure that homeless families receive priority placement.

We will immediately set up effective monitoring procedures to gauge accurately the extent of hidden homelessness throughout London.

### The Scandal of Empty Homes

Action must be taken to bring the thousands of empty homes in London back into use as afford-able housing. We will encourage boroughs to establish clear strate-gies for tackling empty properties with a specific officer to enforce this and maintain a local register of empty homes. We will also en-courage boroughs to enter into voluntary agreements with empty property owners, to renovate their properties for affordable rent and the piloting of empty property management orders across Lon-don. This is where boroughs take control of properties if owners refuse assistance or have dem-onstrated reluctance in bringing their properties back into use.

### Thames Gateway

The project to develop the Thames Gateway in East London offers an unprecedented oppor-tunity to boost economic growth and bring about environmental improvement and urban renewal to an area in desperate need of regeneration. Liberal Democrats fully support the push to take advantage of the huge quanti-ties of brown-field, previously industrial land and to convert its potential by building thousands of much-needed homes and creating employment opportunities for new and existing residents.

However, we have some serious reservations about the way in which the project is currently being managed. A multiplicity of agencies and organisations has developed in the Gateway, with overlapping responsibilities and uncertain remits, creating layers of unnecessary and potentially conflicting bureaucracy. No prop-er consideration has been given to working relationships between the myriad organisations in exist-ence, with a glaring and worrying lack of authoritative leadership and direction. It remains totally unclear how the Government’s proposed Unitary Development Corporation for the Gateway will bring additional benefits. Liberal Democrats suspect that this will only add to the confusion and the duplication of efforts. We believe that the London Development Agency should retain strategic control for the Thames Gateway area so that planning and progress can continue unhindered – par-ticularly on those projects related to London’s Olympics bid.

Liberal Democrats remain un-convinced that there are enough channels for public and com-munity representation and par-ticipation. There is an absence of transparency and little certainty about who to contact to get in-volved, express comments or raise queries. Local accountability is conspicuously missing.

Finally there are concerns about the adequacy of the fund-ing package for the Gateway. The sum in February 2203, extends only until 2006. We will push the Government for a longer-term resource commitment to enable proper planning. This will also ensure that in future years, bor-oughs in the Thames Gateway are not saddled with any new financial burdens as a result of insufficient central investment.

If the Thames Gateway is to be a success, there needs to be co-ordinated and joined up thinking which can tackle all of these issues in a strategic way rather than the piecemeal and juxtaposed approach, which is currently be-ing taken. Clear, accountable, leadership is crucial, as is the full engagement and participation of local communities.

## THAMES GATEWAY

Liberal Democrats have a clear and comprehensive vision for the Thames Gateway. We want to see the development of green and sustain-able communities, with adequate community infrastructure, affordable accommodation, sufficient public transport links and the provision of training and job opportunities that are relevant to and required by local people. We want to see communities rather than just construction – and this involves proper planning with the involvement of transport, health, edu-cation and a whole variety of public services and utilities.

# A CLEANER GREATER LONDON

The quality of our environment is an issue that impacts directly on all Londoners. It is worrying, therefore, that London fairs badly compared to other European cities in terms of its environmental track record. The capital languishes far behind many other European cities. London recycles only 9% of its waste and our air pollution is worsening. 70% of Londoners now perceive the capital’s air quality as a problem. The current Mayor has done little to demonstrate to us that the environment is one of his main concerns. London’s streets remain dirty and its major roads are devoid of greenery.

It’s time that making London a green city was taken seriously so that future generations can enjoy its benefits. Liberal Democrats will continue to be the champions of environmental issues, not shying away from such major challenges as global warming, energy efficiency and waste management, and clearly addressing these in our strategies. We will insist on the highest standard of green design in planning and development across the capital ensuring that London’s beauty is enhanced, promoted and preserved.

### Home Zones
We support ‘Home Zones’ for all residential side streets of London, restricting the speed limit to 20mph. This will reduce noise pollution and improve safety on the streets for Londoners of all ages.

### Air Pollution

We support Low Emission Zones, from which vehicles causing the most harmful air pollution would be barred at all times. We will maintain pressure on the Government to meet EU air quality requirements for **all** of London, including West London, and provide boroughs with the necessary funding to monitor pollution in their area.

We will work with European colleagues to agree a zero level of VAT on all energy conservation materials.

### Airports
We will take action to reduce noise pollution from low-flying aircraft, using EU legislation as support for appropriate operating restrictions around London’s airports. We will also continue our vigorous campaign for an end to night flights over London and maintain our opposition to the Government’s Heathrow expansion plans.

## A HEALTHIER GREATER LONDON

There is so much that can be done. With London's health services under increasing pressure, many NHS staff feel undervalued and overstretched. With inadequate and unequal funding packages, a lack of care facilities and too few people leading an active lifestyle, the challenge is enormous.
Liberal Democrats are not afraid to tackle this challenge and will incorporate health considerations into all our policies. We will continue to press for a radical shift of resources, bringing them closer to patients and cutting the strings that tie local health services to Whitehall. Using Mayoral influence we will continue to press for

access to good NHS health care, including dentist and GP services, for all communities across London.
Many Londoners are not registered with a GP. They currently have nowhere to go except Accident and Emergency departments. To tackle this we will start discussions for a GP or nurse-led walk in centre at or near major hospitals in London.
In addition we will work together with key health agencies to monitor and tackle inequalities in service provision across the capital – making the case for a London Health Inequalities Fund to target investment where it is most needed. Liberal Democrats will also initiate an urgent

examination into the serious lack of residential and nursing home care in London and feed these results into our campaign for an additional share of resources.
Liberal Democrats see the NHS as a health service, not a sickness service. Health promotion needs to rise up the agenda to address the root causes of ill health and not just treat the symptoms. It is also essential that we have an active approach to promoting healthier lifestyles. To this end we will open up fresh dialogue with London boroughs to facilitate improvements and better access to high quality recreational and sports facilities across London.
We will set up a co-ordinated campaign throughout London, targeting particular communities, encouraging people to stop smoking and offering them advice on where they can get free help to do so. We will not impose a London-wide smoking ban in all public places.
We acknowledge the huge recruitment and retention problems facing health services in London. We will work constructively with London health agencies to tackle the root causes behind this, including investigating targeted housing and transport policies and looking into training and London Weighting issues.

**Good health is a fundamental prerequisite for modern life, without which no one can achieve their potential. # PROSPERITY FOR A GREATER LONDON

Alongside the staggering economic might and wealth of business and the City, London is also home to communities suffering from extreme poverty and social deprivation, with the highest unem- ployment of any region in Britain.

An appalling 30% of London- ers of working age are not in work, with 625,000 working-age adults living in poor households. It is deeply worrying that Inner London has a child poverty rate of 48% – by far the highest figure in Great Britain. London also contains two-thirds of the most deprived housing estates in the country, with more than 63,000 homeless house- holds forced to live in temporary accommodation.

Greater London does not get a fair deal from the Government. Each year, central Government takes up to £17 billion more in taxes from Londoners than it gives back to them in spending on public services. Whilst we all acknowledge that London should contribute to the security and prosperity of the United Kingdom, London’s chroni- cally underfunded infrastructure demands immediate improvements and major investment. It is time to start redressing the balance of payments. Liberal Democrats will not rest until the Government accepts it.

Liberal Democrats will listen to and support Greater London’s entrepreneurs, small businesses and large corporations – we want to directly engage and co-operate with London’s wealth creators. In his term of office, the present Mayor has shown far too little un- derstanding of the importance of the business community, failing to recognise or support their needs. This is a trend that Liberal Democrats will reverse in order to secure fairness for all Londoners.

Liberal Democrats look forward to harnessing and developing more innovative sources of funding and economic activity. We will look to attract alternative sources of fund- ing for London’s major projects. This will include business-sponsored schemes and conducting detailed research on the use of bonds and the benefits of pursuing Land Value Taxation (LVT). We will also promote social enterprise in the capital, and work with businesses throughout London to foster a universal sense of corporate respon- sibility for London’s maintenance and future prosperity.

# WORKING FOR A GREATER LONDON

The establishment of the GLA was heralded as a new era of democratic, regional government for the capital. It is a pity that this promise has not been fulfilled. The GLA, supposedly London's governing office, has been unable to fully live up to expectations. Its potential for innovation and strategic policy creation has been constrained by Government afraid to let London off the leash.

The GLA should be a fully operational regional Assembly, run by accountable, locally elected representatives to which a vast array of responsibilities are devolved. However, this is not the case. The Government has insisted on the maintaining the Government Of- fice for London – a department controlled centrally by officials appointed by the New Labour Gov- ernment. It seems that Londoners are still not trusted to run their own affairs.
The current Mayor’s track record is not much better. He has demonstrated a poor record in properly engaging with Londoners and working constructively with the London boroughs. The Mayor has failed to exert his influence on the European and global stage, missing many opportunities to promote London's benefits and support its international priorities. The Mayor must be a Mayor for those that live and work in Greater London, de- livering real improvements for the city and enhancing the prosperity and quality of life for residents Lon- don-wide – not just a few key groups in central London.
Making London government more open and accessible to eve- ryone is an overriding priority for Liberal Democrats. We believe in decentralised, inclusive decision- making and value the contribution of and co-operation with London's Boroughs. As such, it is our pledge to engage and consult with Lon- doners, listen to their views and incorporate them and their elected representatives at every level.

### Abolishing the Government Office for London
The Greater London Authority is an accountable and elected body with executive power. Its purpose is to provide regional government for the capital. Since its establishment the Government Office for London (GOL) has become a totally unnec- essary layer of bureaucracy. GOL now simply duplicates many of the functions conducted by City Hall and the Boroughs. This is an annual cost to taxpayers of over £15mil- lion. It only remains so that central Government can attempt to steer the direction of London's policies. Liberal Democrats will vigorously campaign for the abolition of GOL. We want to secure better value for taxpayers' money and ensure that London is governed democratically by representatives that Londoners themselves have elected.

London is the largest city in Europe and one of the most pres- tigious and famous places in the world. It needs a political voice to match. Liberal Democrats believe that London's Mayor should be the capital's figurehead – an am- bassador for the city to Europe and the world. As Mayor, Simon Hughes will use his influence to state London's case, pursue its economic priorities and promote it as an ideal place to visit, invest in and do business.

## ONE NUMBER FOR LONDON
Community life would be far easier and less stressful if Londoners had one single telephone number that they could use to access all of the public service information they require. There would be no more confusion over who to contact and queries could be resolved without numerous calls to various departments.
To make this a reality, Liberal Democrats will undertake a detailed investigation of the feasibility of establishing a fast, efficient one- stop help line. Located in London, this would operate 24 hrs a day and always be answered by a real person. Modelled on the tried and tested 311 service in New York, the One-Number system would be customer-service driven, initially providing direct access to all GLA information and services. Following this it would be gradually integrated with Borough call centres to make the service Londonwide and comprehensive.
How you might use it:
*   Registering a complaint about noise, graffiti or abandoned vehicles
*   Planning a Tube or bus journey
*   Making non-emergency police calls
*   Querying opening times of local libraries or leisure centres
*   Checking rubbish collection and recycling schedules
*   Finding out information about London's tourist attractions.
