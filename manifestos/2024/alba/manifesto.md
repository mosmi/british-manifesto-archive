### The UK Political and Economic Straightjacket

Under continued macro economic management by Westminster, Scotland will be condemned to lower than trend economic growth within a UK state which itself is exhibiting lower than trend growth.

In practical terms, this means that the austerity which has bedevilled public finances and household budgets will continue to prevail for the foreseeable future. As the Institute of Fiscal Studies has ruthlessly exposed, both Labour and Tory are conducting a phoney election with phantom manifesto costings.

Pledges to freeze tax rates across 75 per cent of the revenue gathering tax base merely disguises the fact that both parties are relying on fiscal drift on income tax to increase their revenues in the most unequal way possible. Joint commitments to increase defence spending mean that even the most modest advances in social provision - for example abolition of the bedroom tax - cannot be afforded.

The refusal of Labour (and the Liberal Democrats) to mend economic fences with the European Union condemn the UK to the continuing damage of Brexit to the economic fabric of the country.

Monetary Policy remains the preserve of the "independent" Bank of England who have but one instrument of interest rates to meet their one inflation target. This "one club golfer" approach to monetary policy has cost the Treasury £20 billion per annum plus additional interest charges on gilts. As it happens, this is approximately the sum of public finance generally thought to be necessary to put the National Health Service, and related social services, back on track.

However the real damage from sky high interest rates maintained over the last two years are the tens of thousands of small businesses and millions of households whose finances have been wrecked by brooding and mortgage costs.

In short, Westminster Government of monetary policy through the Bank of England has made "finance too proud and industry and the people too poor". We would recommend that this monetary stance is changed to see reasonable interest rates as a proper target of economic policy, not a blunt instrument. Returning interest rates to trend position will free up some £20 billion per annum currently wasted in debt servicing costs.

There is nothing, absolutely nothing, on offer from the British political parties which provides an escape route from this low growth, austerity economic prospectus.

The approach of ALBA is entirely different.

We look forward to a Scotland which embraces the next energy revolution. Scotland has the potential to produce, in the foreseeable future, up to ten times our own power requirements from clean green sources and at a cost which is substantially below prevailing market prices. For example, deep offshore wind is the most economic way to produce big power with projected costs now running at around one third of new nuclear investment.

In that prospect there is the means to secure the public finances through a 20 per cent share in every major field, to revitalise the Scottish industrial base by building the vast structures required in deep water Scottish facilities, and to ensure that communities affected by the developments share in the new energy bounty.

Our objective is to sell Scottish power to markets in the south at average prices of development while offering Scottish consumers and Scottish business access to power at marginal cost. Thus, we can both eliminate fuel poverty in our land of energy plenty and provide a key competitive advantage to energy consuming Scottish business.

One of the successes of the SNP government led by Alex Salmond was to transform the efficiency of public capital spend in Scotland through the Scottish Futures Trust. We will review this further for new economic circumstances including the use of Bond finance in major infrastructure projects.

The Scotland ALBA seeks is an independent, prosperous, social democratic society, engaged with our European partners through EFTA and fruitfully engaged with the wider world in the cause of peace and justice.

In this manifesto we sketch out some of ALBA's key campaigns for Westminster government action which would have revenue implications for Scotland and we identify some of our most important initiatives which we will take into the Scottish Parliament campaign in two years' time.

### Tackling the Cost of Living Crisis

Scotland is facing a real cost of living crisis, one that cries out for real action from both the UK and the Scottish Governments - action which has been sadly lacking to date.

Inflation hit a 40 year high in October 2022 at 11.1%. Rishi Sunak has campaigned on the "success" of lowering inflation but he's taken no action to lower costs that sky rocketed as a result of the high inflationary rises.

Energy costs are pushing more and more households into fuel poverty across the country and especially in rural and island communities. Scotland is an energy rich land but our people are fuel poor. It is absurd.

According to the latest Scottish Housing Condition Survey, 791,000 households (31% of all households) were estimated to be in fuel poverty, of which 472,000 (18.5% of all households) were in extreme fuel poverty.

Fuel poverty and extreme fuel poverty have a strong association with income, with rates increasing as annual household income decreases. For example 89% of households with an annual income less than £15,000 were in fuel poverty compared to 43% of households earning between £15,000 and £24,999 annually.

Tory welfare policies such as the Bedroom Tax and the Two Child Cap on Child Benefits actively push more children into poverty. The Labour Party want to keep these policies in place.

ALBA will act to tackle the cost of living crisis through our targeted five-point plan to address family and child poverty.

The ALBA Party will call for their removal. If the UK Government scrapped the bedroom tax it would immediately free up an extra £70 Million for the Scottish Government to spend on other measures to tackle the cost of living crisis.

A typical British worker will at retirement receive a state pension and other benefits worth around 29% of what they had previously been earning. That compares with an average of 63% in other OECD countries, and more than 80% in Italy and the Netherlands.

The ALBA Party want to see state pensions in an independent Scotland coming into line with other European nations over time.

We propose that the triple lock system should be improved which, over a 5 year period, would result in the State Pension in an independent Scotland rising to over £1000 a year more than the expected UK State Pension.

Women Against State Pension Inequality (WASPI) represents a cohort of women born in the 1950s who have been adversely affected by changes to the state pension age. Recognising the injustice faced by these women, an independent Scotland must take proactive steps to address their grievances.

The ALBA Party will pressure the incoming UK Government to immediately compensate WASPI women.

In addition we propose for this coming year a doubling of the winter fuel payment offering 1 million Scottish pensioners, and households with severely disabled children, increased payments of between £100 and £300 to help with heating.

Scotland is awash with natural resources. We produce five times the oil and gas we require and yet every price rise makes us poorer. We can already keep the lights on using renewable electricity alone.

The price of wind or water has not gone up but Westminster prices Scottish electricity on the international price of gas.

Our problem is that our resources are controlled by international capital, London pockets the revenues and Scots pay the bills. That is why we have huge levels of fuel poverty in our land of energy plenty.

#### To tackle the Cost of Living Crisis the Alba Party will support the following measures:

* Establish an annual £500 payment targeted at every low earnings household in Scotland in receipt of council tax reduction. This will benefit half a million households at a cost of £250 million.
* A £1000 increase to the State Pension through the Scottish Triple Lock.
* Compensation for WASPI women.
* The removal of VAT from energy Bills and the reduction of VAT to 10% for next year, saving the average household £2500.
* Increasing the Scottish Child Payment to £40 per week for all eligible families. This will benefit 400,000 children across 250,000 households at a cost of £275 million.
* Scrapping of the Bedroom Tax and ending the Two Child Benefit Cap.
* Introducing universal free breakfasts for all nursery, primary and secondary pupils and extend free school meals to all primary and secondary pupils. This will cost £350 million.
* Doubling the Educational Maintenance Allowance (EMA) from £30 to £60 per week for all 16-19 year olds in full-time school or college to help close the poverty related educational attainment gap. This would benefit 30,000 young people at a cost of £23 million.
* Guaranteeing free universal access for all children and young people to sport facilities. This could potentially benefit one million young people with a cost (based on a take up rate of 80%) of £206 million - a fraction of the billions lost over time to productivity due to obesity or the hundreds of millions cost to the NHS each year.

* Support high-quality, evidence-based care for children and young people.

* Support the Equality Act 2010 protections against discrimination.

The Alba Party prioritises the rights of women and children in this Election. That is why we urge you to vote for the Alba Party: Yes to Scottish Independence on July 4.

### The NHS and Social Care

When Alex Salmond was First Minister he set Scotland on the path towards having the best performing A&E waiting times across the UK. Prescription charges were scrapped meaning that nobody in Scotland had to choose to avoid treatment in fear of the cost.

Health is devolved but decisions made by the UK Government can impact on Scotland's budget and, by default, Scotland's NHS.

The ALBA Party will always support universal entitlement to the NHS free at the point of need. We will oppose Westminster attempts to privatise NHS services that could adversely impact on Scottish funding levels.

Free Personal Care has been available in Scotland for adults aged 65 or over since 2002. In 1999 when the Sutherland Commission recommended introducing free personal care across the UK only Scotland implemented it. The Health Foundation estimated the cost of introducing free personal care in England in 2015/16 at between £3.1bn and £6.7bn, with a central estimate of £4.3bn. Taking account of inflation this would mean a central estimate of £5.7bn today.

ALBA believe that due to the fiscal constraints of devolution, Scotland does not benefit from the macro economic benefits of investing more in social care. Therefore, ALBA will pressure an incoming Labour Government to follow Scotland's Free Personal Care model which would result in circa £500 Million of additional consequentials coming to the Scottish budget.

Across Scotland many people are concerned that they are unable to make an appointment with their doctor and often spend time in a queue, only to be told there are no appointments left and to try again the following day.

The number of registered patients in Scotland has increased by 2.1% over the past two years and Scotland's population is continuing to age, with a 50% increase in over-60s projected by 2033.

The Scottish Government is not on track to meet its commitment of 800 new GPs by 2027, having added only 271 in the past six years.

ALBA will ensure that an increase in funding will be dedicated to increasing the number of GPs to meet the target of a further 500 required by 2027, and in the NHS to training and recruiting to increase the number of Advanced Nursing Practitioners in the Scottish NHS to 1000.

The present Scottish Government has allowed a new crisis in NHS dentistry to emerge. The expansion in NHS dentistry allowed by the opening of the new dental school in Aberdeen has been dissipated and we are now back to square one with many people, particularly in rural Scotland, finding it impossible to access public dental care.

There were 3,396 registered NHS dentists in March 2010. The number grew to a high of 3,735 in September 2019. In the three years September 2019 - September 2022, the number has dropped back to 3,438, the lowest number since 2011.

ALBA will open a new graduate school of dentistry in the HIU Inverness campus building through its work in the Scottish Graduate Entry Medicine Programme (ScotGEM). This will be backed up by Scotland wide incentives for dental graduates to commit to the NHS.

#### ALBA will:

* Support universal entitlement to the NHS free at the point of need.
* Work to increase Scotland's Advanced Nursing Practitioners to 1000 in the Scottish NHS.
* Prioritise measures to get Scotland back on target to achieve 500 new GPs by 2027.
* Call for free personal care in England at the same level as delivered in Scotland.
* Open a new graduate school of dentistry in the HIU campus in Inverness.

### Saving Grangemouth and the Future of the North Sea

The ALBA Party are deeply concerned about the announcement by Petroineos in November 2023 to begin a transition project which could end oil refining at its Grangemouth complex as early as 2025.

The closing of the refinery threatens the jobs of 500 employees and 2,000 contractors at the plant and could have a potentially devastating impact on the local population, businesses and communities, as well as the economic and social fabric of central Scotland. The multiplier impact of a Grangemouth closure would cost many thousands of skilled jobs across the Scottish economy.

ALBA understand the effect this would have on other key industrial sectors such as plastics and chemicals, is mindful that Grangemouth provides around 70% of the nation's fuel at petrol stations and that it is also the primary supplier of aviation fuel for Scotland's airports.

The ALBA Party wholeheartedly support the workers at Grangemouth and the work of Unite the Union in defending jobs dependent on the refinery. At Westminster, the Alba Party's Depute leader Kenny MacAskill has spearheaded the campaign to Save Grangemouth.

We believe that the perilous position of Grangemouth makes the case for independence and control of our own resources.

We are an energy rich land but our people live in fuel poverty. If the Grangemouth Oil Refinery closes Scotland will become the only top 25 oil producer in the world without a refinery capacity, showing there is a clear need to have Scotland's oil refinery to be aligned with Scotland's oil production.

There must be steps taken to ensure that oil from the Forties pipeline, accounting for 40% of all oil production in the North Sea last year, which is piped to Grangemouth is actually refined there, as well as refining the oil from any new licenced fields such as Rosebank.

Last year's GERS figures showed that North Sea Oil and Gas Revenues were a record high for Scotland with £9.4 billion raised in the past financial year. It would be criminally insane to sacrifice such an overwhelming bounty for Scotland when Scotland's Oil & Gas can be the engine room of an independent Scotland and the resource which is used to fund the development of a Net Zero future.

The Alba Party want to see new fields licensed with a condition for them to be a zero carbon development and this can be achieved by a field by field commitment to carbon capture - a technology ALBA believe Scotland could be the world leader in if the UK Government invested a fraction of oil revenues for a "rapid development of a carbon capture network." It should be a license condition of every new development that they invest in the emerging carbon capture network.

In contrast, the Conservatives see Scottish resources as a cash cow for their tax incentives for the wealthy, Labour's plan to end investment allowances will have a devastating impact on jobs and new investment while the SNP have lost all credibility with their ever changing stance on development. To sustain employment at serious levels the North Sea requires exploration, development and production.

#### ALBA will:

* Fight to Save Grangemouth from closure.
* Support a sustainable future for North Sea Oil and Gas workers.
* Support new Oil & Gas licences with carbon neural requirements placed on them secured by private and public carbon capture investment.
* Oppose the SNP and Labour stance on the future of the North Sea that could cost over 100,000 jobs direct and indirect across the UK.

### EFTA, Single Market and Immigration

The ALBA Party is a pro-immigration party, that recognises the tremendous contribution that New Scots, who decide to make Scotland their home, make to wider society. This has been demonstrated by our continued support for safe and legal routes for refugees and asylum seekers and our opposition to the UK Government's inhumane asylum policy.

The ALBA Party's stance on immigration has two primary drivers: firstly that we believe Scotland needs to welcome more migrants to help sustain public services as we have a declining population; and secondly to align to our position that an independent Scotland should seek immediate membership of the European Free Trade Association (EFTA) leading to access to the European Economic Area and the Single Market.

Member states' citizens enjoy freedom of movement in each other's territories in accordance with the EFTA convention. EFTA nationals also enjoy freedom of movement in the European Union (EU). Currently, in practice, EFTA nationals and EU citizens and are not only visa-exempt but are legally entitled to enter and reside in each other's countries.

As a result, de facto, a citizen of an EFTA country can live and work in all the other EFTA countries and in all the EU countries, and a citizen of an EU country can live and work in all the EFTA countries. ALBA believe that Scottish citizens should enjoy this right and that citizens of all EFTA and EU countries should be free to come to live and work in Scotland.

The Windsor House Agreement, which allows for Northern Ireland to be both member of the European Single Market and part of the UK, provides a framework for how an independent Scotland can be members of EFTA but still retain an open land border with the remainder of the UK.

Successfully negotiated, an independent Scotland's membership of EFTA and the EEA accompanied by an agreement similar to Northern Ireland will result in a 5 per cent boost to Scottish GDP.

### Connecting Scotland

The Alba Party recognises that road infrastructure in Scotland is in deplorable condition and needs to be brought up to 21st century standards. This is particularly true of rural Scotland.

Fixing the roads has reached crisis point and requires national action to assist local authorities. When finances are under pressure local authorities often sideline maintenance budgets which has the effect of passing on the cost of vehicle damage to the driver and insurances companies.

There is also a major safety issue on the condition of Scotland's roads. Road defects, especially potholes, have been blamed for 15 deaths and over 700 injuries over the last ten years according to figures obtained from Police Scotland.

The amounts estimated to be paid in compensation now runs to millions while the estimate for fixing the backlog on current costings is £1.3 billion.

However there are solutions. Modern technology can reduce the costs of repair by up to 80 percent.

Spray injection machines such as the Roadmaster can be used as can a range of new more water resistant material for relaying roads.

ALBA propose a national pothole task force, which local authorities can bid into for a fraction of the price of normal procedures. The contracts will be long term, not short term, the machinery and materials will sharply reduce costs and within 5 years the backlog will have been tackled and solved.

The North Coast 500 is particularly significant as it carries a large volume of tourist traffic, which is vital to the economy of the area. Around 40 per cent of the North Coast 500 is single-track and as the tourists enjoy the beautiful scenery there are serious problems caused by the disruption to local people going about their daily business.

Trunk roads in the Highlands are vital routes for trade, commerce and tourism. The A9, A96 and A82 are the main arteries that are used by the large trucks carrying goods into the Highlands. There are also a lot of lorries carrying timber and oversized loads being transported to the wind farms on the roads in the north.

We believe that the dualling of the A9 and A96 is long overdue and that the Scottish Government who have failed lamentably to keep their previous commitments can ensure that the projects are delivered on target. The SNP are entitled to no support north of the Highland line until these commitments made are properly redeemed.

Alba believe that the Scottish Government's support for a third runway at Heathrow airport has not been matched with tangible economic benefits to Scotland. Alba will oppose a third runway at Heathrow Airport and instead lobby for investment in Glasgow, Aberdeen, Edinburgh and Inverness Airports to ensure that Scotland is competitive with comparable European cities.

We recognise that many people in Scotland require to use cross border rail services and the level of service is all too often unacceptable for people in Scotland. We will advocate that the UK Government ends the current franchises and brings cross border rail services back into public ownership.

#### ALBA will:

* Demand an increase to Scotland's capital budget to bring forward the targeted completion of the duelling of the A9.
* Establish a National Pot Hole Taskforce to eliminate the repair backlog over five years.
* Oppose a third runway at Heathrow airport and instead advocate for expansion of Scottish services.
* Support public ownership of cross border rail services.
